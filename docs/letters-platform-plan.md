# Between Homes — Anonymous Letters for International Students

> Working name. Alternatives: *Far Letters*, *Halfway Home*, *Postmarked*, *Dear Somewhere*.

**One line:** A quiet place where international students write anonymous letters about what's weighing on them, and get a kind, human reply from someone who has been there. No likes, no feed, no judgment.

---

## 1. The problem

International students carry a specific kind of weight: homesickness, culture shock, visa and money anxiety, family pressure, language barriers, isolation, discrimination, part-time work stress, and the feeling that "everyone back home thinks I'm living the dream." Surveys keep pointing the same way. Australian studies report very high loneliness and lower social support among international students, and US Healthy Minds data shows anxiety and depression rising among them over the last decade.

They often:
- Don't want to burden family back home.
- Don't have close friends yet in the new country.
- Find campus counselling slow, culturally unfamiliar, or stigmatised.
- Feel unable to express themselves fully in a second language.

Existing places to "get it off your chest" are either **public and judgmental** (Reddit, X, TikTok comments), **chaotic and risky** (feed-style vent apps), **real-time and high-pressure** (live listener chats), or **slow and generic** (letter exchanges not built for this group).

## 2. Competitive landscape

| Product | What it does well | Where it falls short (our opening) |
|---|---|---|
| **Letters to Strangers** (nonprofit letter exchange) | Warm concept, therapy-informed, human moderation | Manual moderation takes ~1–2 days before a match; generic audience; volunteers mostly untrained |
| **Sincerely** (app) | Anonymous letters, topic filters, trigger warnings, report/block, 18+ | Generic audience, app-only, nightly batch; not built around students' specific context or languages |
| **Dear Stranger** (Oregon Humanities, postal) | Slow, thoughtful, very human | Physical mail, one region only, weeks per exchange |
| **Reddit** (r/offmychest, r/internationalstudents) | Huge audience, fast | Public, karma-driven, harsh comments, throwaway accounts, nothing stops cruelty |
| **Vent / TalkLife** (feed apps) | Fast, community feel | Criticised by safeguarding groups for a "vacuum of negative emotions" and mostly report-based moderation; feed mechanics reward performance |
| **7 Cups** (live listeners) | Real-time support | Live chat is high-pressure; you have to be "on" right now |
| **Campus counselling** | Professional | Waitlists, stigma, cultural mismatch, office hours |

**What nobody does well, all at once:**
1. Built *specifically* for international students: their topics, their time zones, their languages.
2. **A reply is guaranteed**, because an unanswered vent hurts more than not writing.
3. **The writer controls the kind of reply** ("just listen" vs "advice please").
4. Replies are **checked before they're delivered**, so cruelty never reaches the writer.
5. **Write in your mother tongue**, read replies in it too.
6. **Local crisis help for the country you're studying in**, which international students usually don't know.

These six things are our edge. Everything else is polish.

## 3. Product principles

1. **Nothing is performed.** No likes, follower counts, view counts, streaks or public profiles. Nothing to compare yourself against.
2. **Slow on purpose.** Letters, not chat. Writing slowly helps people process; reading slowly helps people reply well.
3. **The writer is in charge.** They choose who can reply, what kind of reply, how many, and can delete everything at any time.
4. **Kindness by design, not by hope.** The system makes a good reply easy and a cruel one impossible to deliver.
5. **Anonymous for real.** Collect the minimum. What we don't store can't leak.
6. **Not therapy, and honest about it.** A bridge to professional help, not a replacement.

## 4. Core experience

### 4.1 Writing a letter (the writer)
1. Land on the home page. No sign-up wall. A single prompt: *"What's on your mind? No one will know it's you."*
2. Write the letter (any language). Gentle prompts if they freeze: "What happened today?", "What do you wish someone back home knew?"
3. Choose **what you need**:
   - 🫂 *Just listen*: no advice, only "I hear you."
   - 🧭 *Advice welcome*
   - 🌍 *Tell me you've been there*: share your own experience
4. Optional **topic tags**: homesick · visa/immigration · money · studies · family pressure · loneliness · culture shock · relationships · work · discrimination · identity · other.
5. Optional **privacy check**: we spot names, university names, phone numbers or emails and offer to blur them ("You mentioned *University of X*. Want to hide it?").
6. Choose visibility:
   - **Private** (default): only the matched repliers see it.
   - **Open letter**: also readable in a public, anonymous archive (with consent) so others feel less alone.
7. Send. A small animation: the envelope gets a stamp and "leaves." Show: *"Someone will write back. Usually within a day."*
8. They get a **letter key**: a short, readable passphrase (e.g. `amber-river-quiet-lantern`), saved on their device automatically, shown once to copy. No email needed. Optional email or push notification if they want to be told when a reply arrives.

### 4.2 Receiving a reply
- Replies arrive like mail: "You have a letter 💌".
- Each reply can be:
  - **Thanked privately** (the replier sees "your letter helped someone"; nobody else does).
  - Marked **"this helped"** (feeds our quality metrics and routing).
  - **Reported / hidden** with one tap.
- Writer can send **one follow-up** to a reply ("Thank you, it got better") to close the loop, which is huge for repliers' motivation. Still no open-ended DMs.
- **Close the letter** when done, or **burn it** (permanent delete of letter + replies).

### 4.3 Replying (the replier)
- Anyone can reply after a 3-minute **"How to reply kindly"** onboarding and a pledge:
  - Reflect what you heard before anything else.
  - Respect what they asked for (listen / advice / experience).
  - Share, don't preach. No "you should just…", no religion or politics pushing, no diagnosing.
  - Never ask for contact details or identifying info.
- Repliers pick **experience tags** (not identity): "moved countries alone", "dealt with a visa refusal", "worked while studying", "first-gen student", "lost someone while abroad". Used for matching, never displayed with a name.
- **No browsing feed.** Repliers press "Give me a letter" and get one letter, matched by language, topic, experience tags and *letters waiting longest first*. This prevents cherry-picking and guarantees replies spread fairly.
- While writing, a **kindness assistant** (AI) gives quiet, optional nudges: "This sounds like advice, but they asked to just be listened to." It never writes for them. Human words matter.
- Before delivery, every reply passes **moderation** (see §6). Writers never see a reply that fails.
- Repliers have a private page: letters answered, thank-yous received. No public score.

### 4.4 The guarantee: every letter gets a reply
The worst outcome is opening up and hearing silence. So:
- Matching queue prioritises letters with zero replies, oldest first.
- If a letter hits ~18h unanswered, it's escalated to **Keepers**: a small pool of trusted, trained volunteer repliers (e.g. senior international students, peer mentors).
- Target: **90% of letters answered within 24h, 100% within 48h.**

### 4.5 Multilingual by default
- Write in any language. Replies can be written in any language.
- Each side sees the letter in their language, with a "view original" toggle.
- Matching prefers same-language repliers first, then falls back to translated.
- Launch UI in a handful of languages based on your first community (e.g. English, Hindi, Mandarin, French, Spanish, Arabic, Portuguese, Nepali, Vietnamese). Your existing portfolio already has a `translations.ts` pattern to reuse.

### 4.6 Crisis-aware, location-aware help
- An AI classifier screens each letter for self-harm or suicide risk **before** it goes out.
- If risk is detected, the writer immediately sees a calm, non-alarming card: *"It sounds like things are really heavy right now. You deserve to talk to someone tonight."* With:
  - Helplines for the **country they're studying in** (they pick it once, stored only on device), plus their **home country**.
  - International student-specific lines where they exist (many universities run them).
- Their letter **is still sent** (prioritised to Keepers). Blocking it would feel like rejection.
- We never auto-contact authorities; we are not equipped to and it would destroy trust. Be explicit about this in the Terms.

### 4.7 Small, warm extras (phase 2+)
- **Letters to home**: write the letter you can't send to your parents. Never delivered, just kept (or burned).
- **Letter to future me**: delivered back to you in 1, 3 or 6 months.
- **Open Letters archive**: anonymous letters + best replies (with consent from both), browsable by topic. "Visa refusal" with 40 letters and kind replies is powerful proof you're not alone, and great organic SEO.
- **Time-zone moments**: "Your letter was read in Lagos at 2 a.m." (city-level only, opt-in).
- **Seasonal prompts**: exam season, first winter abroad, festivals away from home (Diwali, Lunar New Year, Eid, Christmas).

## 5. Anonymity & privacy design

| Decision | Why |
|---|---|
| No account required to write; **letter key** (hashed server-side) instead of username/password | Lowest barrier, nothing to identify you |
| Email optional, **only for notifications**, stored encrypted in a separate table, deletable anytime | Keeps identity separate from content |
| Random **pen name per letter** ("A friend in a cold city") instead of a persistent handle | Prevents people linking your letters together |
| PII detector before sending (names, universities, phone, email, social handles) | International communities are small; a university name + home country can identify someone |
| Contact info (emails, numbers, @handles, links) **auto-stripped** from replies | Blocks grooming, scams, off-platform pressure |
| IPs used only for rate-limiting/abuse, kept ≤ 7 days, never joined to letters | Minimise what can leak or be subpoenaed |
| No ad trackers, no third-party analytics; self-hosted, privacy-respecting analytics only (e.g. Plausible/Umami) | Trust is the product |
| **Burn** = real deletion from DB + backups rotation | "Delete" must mean delete |
| Clear, plain-language privacy page: *what we store, for how long, who can see it* | Most competitors bury this |

## 6. Safety & moderation

The **replies** are the risk, not the letters. People opening up are vulnerable; the attack surface is the person replying.

**Layered pipeline:**
1. **Rate limits & friction:** new repliers limited to e.g. 3 replies/day; onboarding + pledge required.
2. **Automatic screening (AI) on every reply before delivery:**
   - Harassment, slurs, mockery, sexual content, religious/political proselytising, scams, contact info, "just get over it"-style dismissiveness.
   - Outcome: `deliver` / `send back to replier with a gentle note` / `hold for human review`.
3. **Human review queue** for borderline cases and anything reported. Target < 4h.
4. **Trust levels:** repliers start "new" (stricter screening + more human review), move to "trusted" after N well-received replies with no reports. Bad actors get silently shadow-limited, then removed.
5. **Writer tools:** hide, report, "don't match me with this replier again".
6. **Letters screened too:** for crisis risk (§4.6) and for abuse (people "venting" hate at a group).
7. **Moderator wellbeing:** rotations, content blurring by default, debrief channel. Moderators burn out; plan for it.

**Policies to write before launch:** Community Guidelines, Terms (18+ only, not therapy, no emergency service), Privacy Policy (GDPR/UK GDPR-ready if you have EU/UK users; adapt for your country), Moderation Policy, Law-enforcement request policy.

## 8. MVP scope (what to build first)

Build the **smallest thing that proves the core loop: write, matched reply, "this helped."**

**In MVP**
- Write a letter (with "what I need" + topic tags) and a letter key.
- Reply flow: onboarding + pledge, "Give me a letter", reply editor with guidance.
- AI moderation of replies before delivery + human review queue (admin page).
- Crisis detection on letters + helpline card (start with 5–10 countries where your users study).
- Inbox: read replies, thank, "this helped", report, burn.
- PII blur suggestions; contact info stripping.
- Email/web-push notifications (optional).
- Keepers escalation (can be manual at first: you get a list of unanswered letters).
- English UI + write in any language, with translate-to-read.

**Not in MVP** (resist the urge): Open Letters archive, letters-to-home, future-me letters, full UI translations, mobile apps, university dashboards, matching by experience tags (start with language + topic).

## 9. Suggested tech stack

You already build React + TypeScript + Vite and deploy via Docker/Nginx to an LXC with a self-hosted GitHub runner. Reuse that.

| Layer | Recommendation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Frontend | **React + TypeScript + Vite** (+ React Router) as a PWA | Same as your portfolio; framer-motion for the envelope moments |
| Backend API | **Node + TypeScript with Hono or Fastify** | Small, fast, easy to self-host |
| Database | **PostgreSQL** (+ Drizzle or Prisma ORM) | Run in Docker on your LXC; nightly encrypted backups |
| Jobs/queue | Postgres-based queue (e.g. pg-boss) | Moderation, escalation timers, notifications; no extra infra |
| AI | An LLM API for moderation, crisis screening, kindness nudges and translation (e.g. a small, fast Claude model for screening) | Keep a human in the loop for anything borderline |
| Notifications | Web Push + optional transactional email (Resend/Postmark) | Email stored encrypted |
| Analytics | Self-hosted Umami or Plausible | No cookies, no third parties |
| Hosting | Your LXC + Docker Compose + Nginx + Cloudflare in front (TLS, DDoS, bot protection, Turnstile CAPTCHA) | Cheap; scale later |

**Faster alternative:** Supabase (Postgres + auth + row-level security + edge functions) if you'd rather not run a backend yourself. The trade-off: less control over data location and deletion.

**Recommendation:** keep this as a **separate repo** from your portfolio. Your portfolio can link to it as a featured project.

### Core data model (first draft)

```
writer        (id, key_hash, notify_email_enc?, push_sub?, created_at)
letter        (id, writer_id, body, language, need: listen|advice|experience,
               topics[], visibility: private|open, pen_name, risk_level,
               status: queued|answered|closed|burned, created_at)
replier       (id, auth_ref, trust_level, languages[], experience_tags[], pledged_at)
assignment    (id, letter_id, replier_id, assigned_at, expires_at)
reply         (id, letter_id, replier_id, body, language,
               moderation: pending|delivered|returned|held|rejected,
               helped: bool?, thanked: bool?, created_at)
follow_up     (id, reply_id, body, created_at)          -- one per reply
report        (id, target_type, target_id, reason, status, created_at)
moderation_event (id, target_type, target_id, verdict, model|human, notes, created_at)
```

## 10. Metrics that matter

- **North star:** % of letters where the writer marked a reply **"this helped."**
- Time to first reply (median, p90); % answered within 24h / 48h.
- % replies blocked or returned by moderation (should fall as repliers learn).
- Reports per 1,000 replies; time to resolve a report.
- Writers who come back and write again within 30 days.
- Writers who later become repliers ("pay it forward" rate). This is your growth engine.
- Crisis cards shown and helpline taps (aggregate only).

## 11. Launch plan

**Phase 0: Validate (2 weeks, no code)**
- Talk to 10–15 international students. Ask: where do you vent now? What stopped you last time? What would make you trust an anonymous site?
- **Concierge test:** a simple form ("write a letter"); you and 5 trusted friends reply by email within 24h. Measure: do people write? Do they come back?

**Phase 1: MVP build (6–8 weeks)**
- Week 1–2: data model, API, letter key, write flow.
- Week 3–4: reply flow, matching queue, inbox.
- Week 5: AI moderation + crisis screening + admin review queue.
- Week 6: notifications, PII blur, policies, polish + accessibility.
- Week 7–8: private beta with 30–50 writers and 15–20 seeded Keepers.

**Phase 2: Grow (months 3–6)**
- Launch at your university and 2–3 others: international student offices, student associations, cultural societies, WhatsApp/Discord groups.
- Content: (consented, anonymised) open letters as Instagram/TikTok cards. "A letter from someone's first winter abroad" is shareable and drives people to write.
- Add: Open Letters archive, full multilingual UI, experience-tag matching, letters-to-home, future-me.

**Phase 3: Sustain (6+ months)**
- Partner with universities: they promote it; in return they get **anonymised, aggregated** wellbeing trends ("visa anxiety letters up 3× in March"), only with minimum group sizes so no one can be identified.
- Funding paths: university partnerships, student-wellbeing grants, a "support the post office" donation model. **Never ads, never selling data.**

**Solving the cold start (the biggest risk):**
- Seed with Keepers before launch so the reply guarantee holds on day one.
- After someone receives a reply, gently invite them: *"Someone was there for you. Want to be there for someone?"*
- Aim for ~1 active replier per 3 active writers.

## 12. Risks & how we handle them

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Someone in crisis | Pre-send screening, local + home helplines, Keepers priority, clear "not an emergency service" messaging |
| Trolls / cruel replies | Pre-delivery moderation, trust levels, no browsing feed, rate limits, shadow-limiting |
| Predators / scammers | Contact-info stripping, no DMs, no links, 18+, pattern detection on repliers |
| Not enough repliers | Keepers, pay-it-forward invites, university volunteer programmes |
| De-anonymisation in small communities | PII blurring, coarse-only location, per-letter pen names |
| Data breach | Minimal data, encrypted emails, hashed keys, short IP retention, real deletion |
| Moderator burnout | Rotations, blurred content, small trusted team, clear escalation |
| Legal liability | Clear Terms, 18+, privacy policy, get a short legal review before public launch |
| AI mistakes | AI only flags or nudges; humans decide on anything borderline; regular audits of false positives |

## 13. Open decisions

1. **Name & identity.** Pick a name and check domain + social handles.
2. **First community.** Which university/country do we launch in? This decides the first helplines and languages.
3. **Team.** Solo or with others? Moderation needs at least 2–3 trusted people from day one.
4. **Hosting region & law.** Where users are (EU/UK/Canada/Australia/US) decides privacy obligations.
5. **Replier eligibility.** Anyone 18+, or only verified students (e.g. a one-time `.edu`/`.ac` email check, not stored)? Verified-only is safer and more relatable; open is bigger.
